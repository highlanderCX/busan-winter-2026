The plan

Six winter days on Korea's southern coast — temples over the sea, night markets, the biggest spa in the country, and a very good excuse to eat crab.

BASE Haeundae WINTER 2–9°C SUNSET ~17:15 PACE Family-sane
01
Day one FRI · 18 DEC

Land soft, first sea air

Arrive Gimhae, cross the city to Haeundae, and let the beach do the jet-lag recovery. Early night — the big days are ahead.

Kid-friendlySeafood dinnerLight day

  1. Morning departure lands you at Busan's Gimhae airport around midday. Grab T-money cards and a prepaid SIM or eSIM at the arrivals hall before leaving.

    At the airport
    • T-money card at any CU / 7-Eleven — ₩4,000 card, then top up ~₩20,000 each.
    • KT / SKT tourist SIM or eSIM — 5-day data pass ~₩20,000. Book online ahead for pickup.
    • K-ETA: required for visa-free visitors — apply at least 72 h before departure.

    Verify entry rules & flight times ~1 month out.

  2. Two ways across town — the scenic-cheap way and the tired-family way:

    • Metro (₩1,700/pp): Gimhae Light Rail to Sasang, change to Line 2 (green), ride to Haeundae. ~70 min with one easy transfer. Lifts at all stations.
    • Taxi (₩35–45k): ~40 min direct to the hotel. Split four ways, this is often the smart arrival move with luggage and a kid.

    Use Naver Map or KakaoMap — Google Maps is unreliable for Korean transit directions.

  3. Base for all six nights: a family room in Haeundae — walkable to the beach, Line 2 at the door, and close to the metro for every day trip in this plan.

    • Unpack once. Laundry, kettle, extra blankets — you know the drill.
    • Convenience-store raid: banana milk, kimbap, hand warmers for tomorrow.

  4. Winter sunset lands ~17:15 — go anyway. The empty beach, the bronze statue of the mermaid, and the lights coming on over the East Sea is the perfect arrival scene, and it's 5 minutes from anywhere in Haeundae.

    • Loop the Dongbaekseom boardwalk (flat, 20 min, kid-easy) past Nurimaru APEC House.
    • Bungeoppang & hotteok stands near the beach entrance — first snack of the trip.
    • Wind off the water is no joke in December — layers, hats, hand warmers.

  5. Two solid first-night options, both a short stroll from the beach:

    • Millak raw fish market (민락회타운): pick a live fish downstairs, they slice it upstairs. Hoe + maeuntang (spicy fish stew) for the table. ~₩30–40k pp.
    • Grilled eel street: jangeo-gui over charcoal — rich, warm, very Busan.
    • Kid fallback: kalguksu (knife-cut noodle) house — warming and mild.

    Cash helps at market stalls; restaurants take cards.

02
Day two SAT · 19 DEC

Village of color, market of fish, bridge of light

The classic south-Busan loop: Gamcheon's painted alleys, the old-town markets, then the Gwangalli drone show after dark. Big day — start early.

Stamp tourStreet food crawlFree evening show

  1. Busan's postcard: a hillside of houses painted every color, stacked above the sea. Go before 10:00 — by late morning it's wall-to-wall tour groups.

    With a kid
    • Buy the stamp-map (₩2,000) at the info centre — collecting all 9 stamps turns the alleys into a treasure hunt.
    • Little Prince & Fox statue at the top — everyone queues for a photo.
    • Stairs everywhere; wear real shoes, skip the stroller.
    Getting there
    • Line 2 to Seomyeon → Line 1 to Toseong (토성) exit 6 → green village minibus 2-2 / 2 / 1-1 uphill (~15 min), or a ~₩6k taxi from the station.
    • Entry is free; optional map at the tourist info desk.

  2. Lunch is a walking buffet through Busan's old movie district — this is where Korean street food gets serious.

    • BIFF square: seed hotteok (syrupy, nutty — the original), tteokbokki, sundae, eomuk skewers. Share everything, ~₩8–12k all-in.
    • Gukje Market: a maze of stalls since the Korean War — snacks, socks, souvenirs, and the famous alley food hall.
    • Mum's friend will love the vintage corner shops under the arcades.

    Bus 2 from Gamcheon base, or ~₩7k taxi.

  3. Korea's largest seafood market, right on the port. Even if nobody buys anything, the ground floor — tanks of everything the East Sea produces — is an aquarium you walk through.

    • Still peckish? Upstairs sashimi hall: point, they slice, you eat by the window over the harbor (~₩25k pp).
    • Dried seafood floor: great souvenir squid and laver, prices drop if you buy two.
    • Live octopus (sannakji) is the brave-parent order of the day.

  4. Two big markets in one afternoon is a lot with a kid. Options:

    • Hot chocolate at a Nampo-dong café (the alley cafés behind the theater are excellent).
    • Or taxi back to Haeundae (₩15k, 25 min) for a proper rest before the night show — recommended.

  5. Dinner on the beach with the full span of Gwangan Bridge lit up over the water — then hundreds of drones draw shapes in the sky above it. It's free, it's on Saturdays, and it's the single best evening in Busan.

    • Line 2 to Geumnyeonsan (금련산) exit 1, or Gwangan (광안) exit 3 → 8-min walk to the sand.
    • Dinner: the sashimi alley behind the beach (광안리 횟집거리) — hoedeopbap (sashimi rice bowl) ~₩15k, kid-friendly udon next door.
    • M Drone Light Show: typically two shows Saturday night (~19:00 & ~21:00, 10 min each). Times shift seasonally — confirm on the Busan city site that week.
    • Stand on the sand facing the bridge; arrive 30 min early for a clear view.
03
Day three SUN · 20 DEC

Temple on the sea, crab for lunch, spa till dark

Busan's only seaside temple in the morning, winter crab at the source, then four floors of hot baths at the country's biggest spa. The mums' favorite day.

Spa afternoonWinter crabSpa kids' zone

  1. Most Korean temples hide in mountains. This one spills down the rocks straight into the sea — prayer halls, a golden laughing Buddha, and a long stair of 108 steps beside the water.

    • Line 2 to Haeundae exit 7 → bus 181 to Yonggungsa stop, then a 10-min downhill walk; taxi from Haeundae ~₩12k, 20 min.
    • Go early: tour buses arrive from 10:00 onward.
    • Bundle up — the headland is fully exposed to the sea wind.
    • Kid quest: count the zodiac animal statues lining the stairway (12, one per birth year).

    Check opening hours before going — they vary by season.

  2. December is deep daegae (snow crab) season, and the fishing town of Gijang — 15 minutes up the coast — is where Busanites go for it. Whole crab steamed at the table, legs snipped with scissors, roe fried rice to finish.

    • The crab restaurant alleys sit near Jangseungpo / Gijang port — taxi ~₩15k from the temple, or Line 1 to Gijang station then short taxi.
    • Order 대게찜 (steamed set) for the table — market price, typically ₩50–70k for a crab that feeds 2–3, plus free banchan.
    • Finish with 볶음밥 (crab roe fried rice) made in the shell — non-negotiable.

    If crab feels heavy, the seafood shacks at Cheongsapo do grilled shellfish instead.

  3. The world's largest department store (a Guinness record, genuinely) with four floors of jjimjilbang underneath. This is the plan for the cold afternoon — everyone gets what they want.

    • Spa Land (₩25–35k / 4 hrs): 22 hot spring baths, 13 themed sauna rooms (salt room, clay ball room, ice room), nap zones, a snack bar. Book online — weekends sell out.
    • For the kid: the baths are family hours (mixed, swimwear) at set times — check the board; the game corner keeps teens busy.
    • For the mums: the outdoor foot-bath garden with hot spring water, facing the city. This is the moment.
    • For whoever's shopping: Centum City above — Korean beauty, kids' floor, winter food hall.
    • Line 2 to Centum City (센텀시티) exit 10 — you're inside before you feel the cold.

  4. No need to travel again — eat where you are:

    • Shinsegae food hall (B1) — Korean comfort stalls, quick and warm.
    • Or the restaurant floors: samgyetang (ginseng chicken soup) is the right winter order.
    • Back to Haeundae on Line 2 in 12 minutes; early night.
04
Day four MON · 21 DEC

Mountain temple, fortress wall, sky capsule

Up into Busan's sacred mountain for a 1,300-year-old temple and winter views, then back down to the coast for the sky-train at sunset. Adventure day for the kid, fresh air for everyone.

Sky capsuleTemple photo opsModerate walking

  1. Busan's great mountain temple, founded in 678 — stone gates, lantern-lined courtyards, and a valley where wild wisteria grows in spring. In December the last red maples can hang on at altitude.

    • Line 1 to Beomeosa (범어사) exit 5 → free temple shuttle bus (runs frequently) or a 15-min uphill walk past the shops.
    • Pick up yakgwa (honey cookies) from the hill shops — temple-area specialty, kid-approved.

  2. Walk the temple grounds slowly: the One Pillar Gate, the three-story pagoda, the main hall where you can watch monks chant. Behind the temple, a forest trail climbs to Geumjeongsanseong, Korea's largest mountain fortress wall — even just to the first gate (North Gate, ~25 min uphill) is worth it.

    • Kid fuel: the temple's own temple-food café does simple set lunches (reservations recommended on weekends).
    • The 800-m fortress wall loop is for fit hikers only — with a kid, turn around at the gate.
    • Full cable-car option: from Geumgang Park (금강공원) the cable car climbs to the ridge (~₩12k return) — spectacular winter views if the sky is clear.

  3. Back down the hill for lunch in the Beomeosa restaurant strip — order the local temple-food set or a bubbling baeksuk (whole chicken soup) to shake off the mountain cold.

    • Most places ~₩12–18k pp; the chicken soups serve 2–3 easily.
    • Vegetarian temple sets available — good for the ladies.

  4. The trip's kid-magnet: little pastel pods that crawl along a rail 7–10 m above the coastline, from Mipo to Cheongsapo — sea on one side, cliffs and cafés on the other. In winter, golden-hour departures catch the sunset over the water.

    • Book online 1–2 weeks out (bluelinepark.com or Klook) — December sunset slots sell out. ~₩35–50k per capsule (fits 4).
    • Mipo station: Line 2 Dongbaek exit 1, or walk the coast from Haeundae (~20 min).
    • One-way ride ~30 min; ride out at ~15:30, hot chocolate at Cheongsapo, ride the train back (₩7k) — or sunset capsule both ways.
    • Cheongsapo tip: the lighthouse blinks on right as you arrive — the photo everyone takes.

  5. Easy, warm, and close to bed — the traditional market's food alley does the classics at local prices.

    • Order: dwaeji gukbap (pork-and-rice soup — Busan's signature), shared jeon (savory pancake) if anyone's still hungry.
    • Grab fruit and snacks for tomorrow's theme-park day.
05
Day five TUE · 22 DEC

All-in day at Lotte World, Christmas lights after

An almost-fully-indoor theme park built for exactly this weather, then the Haeundae beach lights switch on for the season. The kid's day — and honestly, the adults' too.

Theme parkIndoors = warmNight market

  1. Line 2 to Jangsan (장산) exit 6, then taxi ~10 min (₩8k) or free/paid shuttle to the park entrance. Leave a little early — you'll want to be at the gates for opening.

  2. Seoul's Lotte World little-sibling, purpose-built indoors — rides, parades, and a whole kids' zone under one giant roof. December means Christmas décor everywhere and near-zero weather risk.

    • Tickets: buy online (Klook / official site) before you fly — often 20–30% off gate price (~₩55–65k adult, kid discounts; check for family bundles).
    • Strategy: at opening, hit the big rides first (Giant Digger, Giant Splash queues grow fast), then drift to the kid zone as crowds build.
    • Lunch inside — the food hall is genuinely decent and not extortionate.
    • Lockers by the entrance for coats; you'll overheat otherwise.
    • Parade around midday / afternoon — check the board at the gate.

  3. Every December the beach promenade becomes a tunnel of lights and installations — trees, a giant tree at the square, light art on the sand. It runs roughly early-December to late-January and switches on around dusk (~17:30).

    • Free, walkable, and 5 minutes from the hotel — perfect low-energy evening.
    • Weeknight evenings are far calmer than weekends.

    Festival dates are announced each autumn — confirm for 2026 before you go.

  4. Final-night choices, all within the Haeundae grid:

    • Korean BBQ night: hanwoo (Korean beef) grill — the celebratory order. ~₩35k pp with soju.
    • Comfort option: seolleongtang (ox-bone soup) — gentle, warm, perfect before a travel day.
    • The mums' toast: hotel-bar nightcap or a Haeundae craft-beer bar — you've earned it.

Day six WED · 23 DEC

Slow morning, last sea coffee, fly home

No alarms. One last look at the water, souvenirs packed, and a clean run to the airport with time to spare.

Departure daySouvenirs

  1. Coffee at a beachfront café watching the morning joggers — the quiet version of the view you met on day one. Check out by late morning; most hotels store bags for free.

  2. One efficient sweep, all near the hotel:

    • Haeundae traditional market: fresh eomuk (fish cakes — vacuum-packed for the flight), sesame oil, seaweed.
    • Convenience stores: the famous triangle-kimbap making-of... actually just pack a few for the plane; honey butter almonds; cute socks (it will happen).
    • K-beauty top-ups at the local Olive Young if the Centum City day wasn't enough.
    • Keep liquids over 100 ml in checked luggage — fish-cake packs are fine either way.
    • Metro: Haeundae → Sasang (~50 min) → Gimhae Light Rail to the airport (~15 min). Budget 90 min door-to-terminal with bags.
    • Taxi: ~40 min, ₩35–45k — the stress-free farewell with luggage and a kid.
    • Tax refund (Global Blue / Global Tax Free counters) if you kept receipts over ₩30k.
    • Airport's not huge: 1.5–2 hrs before departure is comfortable.

Field notes

Before you fly

01December weather

2–9°C, dry and windy on the coast. Sunset ~17:15, so outdoor plans cluster early. Pack down jackets, hats, gloves and a compact umbrella; pocket hand warmers (핫팩) are sold at every convenience store and Daiso for ~₩1,000.

02Getting around

Busan's metro covers this whole plan. T-money card (₩4,000 + top-up) taps metro, bus and taxi. Taxis start ~₩4,800 and are cheap across town. Use Naver Map or KakaoMap — Google Maps doesn't do Korean transit well.

03Money & cards

Cards accepted almost everywhere; market stalls and small restaurants are cash-friendly. Global ATMs at convenience stores. Street food runs ₩2–8k, sit-down meals ₩12–25k pp, crab feast ₩30–40k pp.

04Connectivity

Prepaid tourist SIM or eSIM (~₩20k for 5 days, KT/SKT) — pick up at PUS arrivals or install an eSIM before departure. Hotel and café Wi-Fi is everywhere but you'll want data for maps on the go.

05Entry & help

K-ETA required for visa-free visitors (apply ≥72 h ahead; rules can change — recheck before booking). Tourist hotline 1330 (24 h, multilingual). Emergency: 112 police / 119 fire & ambulance.

06Booking shortlist

Reserve before you fly: Sky Capsule (bluelinepark.com — sunset slots), Spa Land, Lotte World tickets (Klook often cheapest), and any crab-lunch weekend table. Everything else is walk-up.

  • Flights & hotel — 5 nights, one room + rollaway or family twin, Haeundae, on Line 2.
  • K-ETA approvals ×4 — passport valid 6+ months for everyone. ≥72 HRS BEFORE
  • Sky Capsule — book the ~15:30 Mipo→Cheongsapo sunset slot. 1–2 WEEKS OUT
  • Spa Land — Sunday afternoon session ×4. ONLINE, WEEKENDS SELL OUT
  • Lotte World Busan tickets — Tuesday, Dec 22. ONLINE = 20–30% OFF
  • Verify seasonal events — Gwangalli drone show (Sat), Haeundae lighting festival, festival opening hours. WEEK OF THE TRIP
